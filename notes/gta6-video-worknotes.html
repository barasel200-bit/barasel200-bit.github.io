Why fan trailers read as fake in the first three seconds

2026-11-26 · working notes

Fan-made trailers usually fail immediately, and the reason is rarely the render quality. It is the mismatch between camera language and subject. Real game footage moves like a camera operator following a character; generated footage often moves like a camera that knows where the character will be, which reads as a cutscene rather than gameplay.

Fix the first shot before anything else. Put the camera slightly behind and to one side of the subject, add a small amount of handheld drift, and let something pass in front of the lens within the first second. Those three details do more for believability than a higher resolution. Examples and breakdowns of this approach are collected at GTA 6 AI video.

Keep the frame busy at the edges. Game worlds have traffic, pedestrians and signage; generated frames tend to be empty behind the subject, and emptiness is what the eye reads as fake even when it cannot name why.

Finally, cut before the shot resolves. Generated clips tend to settle into a static composition at the end, and cutting a beat early hides the moment when the motion gives out.

Study one real gameplay capture frame by frame and copy its imperfections: the slight overexposure when the camera turns toward the sun, the compression smearing on fast pans. Those artefacts are cheaper to imitate than to render honestly.

Watch the first second on mute, then with sound; the two impressions should agree.

Then show the clip to someone who does not play the game and ask what felt wrong; their answer is usually the detail you stopped seeing.
