Choosing the song before the photos

2026-11-26 · working notes

The usual order is photos first, music later, and it is the wrong way round. A song fixes the length, the tempo and the emotional arc; a photo set fixes none of those. Pick the track, cut it to the length you want, and then choose photos that fit the sections you now have.

A workable structure for a memory video is intro, verse, chorus, second verse, final chorus. The chorus is where the faces go, because that is where people look. Verses carry context: the street, the table, the dog, the hands. If you only have twelve good photos, cut the song to ninety seconds rather than stretching the photos across three minutes. Memories Song is built around this order, and the difference in the first draft is obvious.

Match the motion to the tempo. Slow pans over fast music feel wrong even when nobody can say why, and quick cuts over a slow ballad feel like a slideshow with a heartbeat.

Watch the result once with the sound off. If the sequence still reads as a story, the edit is doing its job and the song is a bonus rather than a crutch.

Leave one silence. A short gap before the final chorus does more emotional work than another photo, and it gives the viewer a moment to recognise the people on screen rather than just watch them go past.

A single slow zoom on the last photo is usually enough; two effects competing for the same moment read as noise.
