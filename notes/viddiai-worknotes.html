Storyboarding a fifteen-second product clip in a text-to-video tool

2026-11-26 · working notes

Fifteen seconds is five shots, not one long take. The structure that survives client review is: a wide establishing shot, a detail, a use shot, a reaction, and a closing packshot. Write those five as separate prompts even if the tool can generate a continuous clip, because separate prompts let you replace one shot without regenerating the rest.

Keep the product description identical across all five prompts, word for word. Small variations in wording produce different packaging, different label colours and different proportions, and continuity errors are the first thing a client notices. A lightweight workbench such as Viddi AI is enough for this stage; the goal is a timed assembly, not a finished film.

Cut on motion, not on beats. Text-to-video outputs rarely have a clean beat to cut to, but they almost always have a moment where the camera settles or the subject turns, and that moment hides a cut better than any music cue.

Render the assembly at low resolution first and watch it on a phone. Fifteen seconds of product footage is watched on a phone by almost everyone who will ever see it, and a shot that reads clearly on a monitor can disappear entirely on a small screen.

Sound design at this stage is a placeholder, not a decision. Drop a single ambient bed under the assembly so the cuts feel intentional, and leave music choices until the picture is locked, because tempo will change after the first client note anyway.

Lock the aspect ratio before the first render, because re-framing five shots at the end costs more than re-rendering them now.
