Keeping image and video drafts in one workspace

2026-11-26 · working notes

Drafts scatter. A thumbnail lives in one tool, a motion test in another, and the note explaining why the third version was rejected lives in a chat. The cost is not storage, it is the ten minutes every morning spent reconstructing what you were doing yesterday.

One workspace for both image and video drafts fixes most of that, provided the workspace keeps the prompt next to the output. A single place for text-to-video and image-to-video work, such as Pavo AI, is useful less because of any single model and more because the history stays in one column you can scroll.

Name drafts by shot, not by date. Shot numbering survives reorganisation and matches the script, while dates become meaningless within a week. Add the seed to the name if the tool exposes it, because the seed is the only reliable way back to a version you liked.

Export the shortlist weekly. Anything that lives only inside a tool is one policy change or pricing update away from disappearing, and a folder of approved stills costs almost nothing to keep.

Review the workspace weekly and delete the dead ends. A history that contains everything becomes unusable, while a history that contains the approved paths and the rejected ones with a one-line reason becomes the project's memory.

Put the shot number in the file name; it is the only label that still makes sense after a week.

A folder of approved exports is the only part of the workspace that survives a change of tool, so treat it as the deliverable and the tool as temporary.
