Fast image drafts for storyboard thumbnails

2026-11-26 · working notes

Storyboards do not need beauty, they need decisions. The job of a thumbnail is to answer three questions: where the camera is, what the subject is doing, and how much of the frame the subject occupies. A fast image generator is good at this precisely because it is careless about detail.

Work at a fixed aspect ratio that matches the final delivery, and refuse to crop later. Generate four thumbnails per shot, pick one, and write the shot number on it before moving on. The temptation is to keep generating until something impressive appears, which is how a ten-minute storyboard becomes an afternoon. A generator like Grok AI Image Generator is fast enough that the limit is your decision speed, not the tool.

Keep the style deliberately flat. Photorealistic thumbnails invite comments about lighting and skin, and those comments are not about the story. Flat colour and simple shapes keep the conversation on framing, which is the only thing a storyboard is for.

When the board is approved, redo the chosen frames at higher fidelity for the client deck, and keep the originals. Clients approve compositions, then forget them; the flat version is the record of what was agreed.

Keep a single reference folder of approved compositions. When a new shot is needed, start from that folder rather than from a blank prompt, and the board keeps its visual grammar instead of drifting toward whatever the model finds easiest today.

Four thumbnails per shot is the right batch size: enough to compare, few enough to choose quickly.
